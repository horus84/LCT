\begin{abstract}
Instruction-tuned language models frequently accommodate the stated political preferences of user personas. However, raw persona-match rates on static benchmarks can conflate a model's fixed political prior with causal persona accommodation. Using a paired counterfactual design on the Anthropic Political Typology Benchmark, we test whether reversing a user's stated political identity causally changes the model's answer, and at what point during a visible rationale (Chain-of-Thought) the persona-aligned answer becomes stable. Evaluating \texttt{Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct}, we isolate true persona-conditioned answer changes from baseline item biases. Furthermore, applying a token-level early-answering intervention across the generated rationales, we plot a commitment trajectory revealing exactly when the persona-induced answer difference stabilises. Our results demonstrate that paired behavioural interventions offer a cleaner estimate of persona sensitivity than group-level raw match rates, and that visible reasoning dynamics can be systematically audited for answer stability.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Instruction-tuned language models exhibit a well-documented tendency to accommodate the stated beliefs of their users, a phenomenon often studied under the umbrella of sycophancy \citep{perez2022discovering,sharma2023towards}. Recent evaluation paradigms frequently rely on static benchmarks where a model's response to a specific persona is evaluated. However, measuring raw persona-match rates across disjoint groups of personas can conflate the model's fixed training prior with genuine, causal accommodation to the user's prompt. 

Concurrently, the adoption of Chain-of-Thought (CoT) prompting \citep{wei2022chain} and natively elicited reasoning traces introduces a visible rationale before the final answer. While this provides an audit trail, prior work \citep{turpin2023language,lanham2023measuring} cautions that these visible rationales may not faithfully represent the internal causal mechanisms driving the final output.

In this paper, we conduct a behavioural benchmark audit using a paired counterfactual design. By exposing the model to the exact same political question under reversed persona identities (e.g., liberal vs. conservative), we cleanly isolate persona-conditioned answer changes. We then employ early-answering interventions to truncate the visible rationale at multiple token percentages, measuring when the persona-conditioned answer becomes stable. 

\paragraph{Claims.}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Raw persona-match rates can conflate fixed model preferences with causal accommodation to a user's stated position.
    \item Paired persona reversal provides a cleaner estimate of persona-conditioned answer changes than comparing distinct persona groups.
    \item For persona-sensitive examples, early-answering interventions reveal when the persona-conditioned answer becomes stable during the visible rationale.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
Our work builds on the literature regarding model sycophancy and the faithfulness of visible reasoning. \citet{perez2022discovering} identified political persona accommodation using model-written evaluations, a benchmark we adapt here. We address recent concerns regarding the construct validity of such benchmarks \citep{iclr2026sycophancy} by restricting our claims to structural persona alignment rather than human-perceived dishonesty, framing the behaviour as social alignment and audience design.

Regarding reasoning faithfulness, \citet{turpin2023language} demonstrated that CoT-prompted models can produce unfaithful explanations that mask biasing features. \citet{lanham2023measuring} introduced the early-answering intervention, showing that truncation of prompted CoT often does not alter the final decision. We extend this methodology by applying token-level truncation specifically to track the emergence of persona-induced differences during the rationale.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Dataset and Paired Intervention}
We adapt 100 items from the Anthropic Political Typology Benchmark. To ensure tight causal control, we construct paired counterfactuals: for each underlying political question, we generate a Prompt~A (liberal persona) and a Prompt~B (conservative persona). The exact question, options, and irrelevant biographical details remain constant; only the explicit political identity phrasing is altered.

\subsection{Decoding and Truncation Conditions}
We evaluate \texttt{Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct} under greedy decoding ($T=0$). For each prompt pair, we generate responses in two conditions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Direct Answer:} The model outputs the option letter directly, without a visible rationale.
    \item \textbf{CoT-Prompted:} The model is instructed to provide a visible rationale inside \texttt{<think>...</think>} tags prior to answering.
\end{itemize}

To measure rationale stability, we apply a token-level early-answering intervention to the CoT traces. We extract the generated rationale, truncate it at specific token percentages (10\%, 50\%, 75\%, 100\%), append a forcing string (\texttt{``Therefore, the final answer is (''}), and generate the final option token.

\section{Conclusion}
By applying a paired counterfactual intervention, we separate fixed political preferences from causal persona accommodation. Our early-answering analysis demonstrates the specific point in the visible rationale where persona-conditioned answers achieve stability. This methodology provides a rigorous framework for auditing social alignment and reasoning dynamics without relying on disjoint group comparisons.

\section*{Limitations}
Structural labels objectively identify whether an answer matches the supplied persona, but they do not establish human-perceived sycophancy, flattery, or social appropriateness. Our interpretation is strictly limited to the benchmark's operational definition of persona alignment. Furthermore, our experiments evaluate a single instruction-tuned model (\texttt{Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct}) on a binary, US-centric political axis. Generalization to other model families or multi-dimensional political typologies requires further investigation. The truncation methodology forces an abrupt context shift, which may independently disrupt generation stability.
